Executive Summary

For large enterprises such as IBM, a cyber attack can cause the following major impacts:

  • Systems and servers compromised — leading to service outages.
  • Exfiltration of sensitive data (IP, customer data, trade secrets) — legal and financial consequences.
  • Brand damage and regulatory scrutiny.
  • Costs for restoration, forensics, customer notification, and potential fines.

Documentation: What to include in your incident report

  1. Incident summary — attack type, timeline, affected systems, initial access vector.
  2. Impact analysis — data types exposed, downtime, customer impact, estimated cost.
  3. Root cause — vulnerability, misconfiguration, compromised credentials, third-party supply chain.
  4. Mitigation actions taken — containment steps, eradication, patches, credential rotations.
  5. Recovery plan — systems restored, validation, monitoring improvements.
  6. Post-incident recommendations — policy, technical controls, training, audits, and governance actions.

Key Technical Controls

  • Adopt a Zero Trust architecture — Identity-first
  • Strong IAM: enforce MFA, least-privilege, and session monitoring — MFA
  • Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR) and Extended Detection & Response (XDR) — EDR/XDR
  • Network segmentation and micro-segmentation — Segmentation
  • Regular patching and vulnerability management program — Patch cadence
  • Secure CI/CD and supply-chain scanning (SBOM) — SBOM
  • Encryption at rest and in transit, with strong key management — KMS
  • Data Loss Prevention (DLP) and exfiltration detection — DLP

People & Process Controls

  • Maintain an Incident Response (IR) runbook and run tabletop exercises regularly (quarterly).
  • Operate a Security Operations Center (SOC) with 24/7 monitoring (in-house or MSSP).
  • Vendor risk management and security SLAs for third parties.
  • Regular security awareness training and phishing simulations for staff.
  • Legal and communications playbooks for breach disclosure and PR management.

Governance & Compliance

  • Board-level cyber reporting and alignment with cyber insurance.
  • Continuous compliance monitoring for applicable regulations (e.g., SOX, GDPR, CCPA).
  • Regular red-team / purple-team adversary emulation exercises.

Quick Checklist (for an IBM-scale enterprise)

  1. Enable MFA on all privileged and administrative accounts.
  2. Rotate and vault credentials; remove unused or stale access.
  3. Deploy EDR and central logging (SIEM) with at least one year of retention.
  4. Segment critical workloads (finance, IP repositories) into isolated zones.
  5. Conduct monthly vulnerability scans and prioritize patching for critical CVEs.
  6. Enforce least-privilege across developers, engineering, and third-party integrations.
  7. Test backups and run IR exercises quarterly; validate recovery procedures.
